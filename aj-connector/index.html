What it actually does

A small add-in sits inside Revit. Press one button and your browser opens with the tools as buttons. Click one, and it works on the model you have open.

The browser is where you click

All the buttons, the search, the results. With enough tools, searching beats hunting through ribbon tabs — that is the honest reason it lives in a browser rather than the ribbon.

One pageSearchable

Revit is where the work happens

The page never touches your model itself. It asks Revit, and Revit does it — on your current view, exactly as if you had pressed a ribbon button. The answer comes back on the page instead of as a popup you have to dismiss.

You install once

New tools arrive on their own. So do fixes inside a tool. Only the add-in itself would ever need installing again, and that will be rare.

Is it safe?

This downloads code and runs it in Revit, so that question deserves a real answer rather than a reassurance.

Every tool carries my signature

Before anything runs, AJ Connect checks the tool against my key — on your computer, not on my website. If a tool was not signed by me, or if one character of it changed after I published it, it is refused. Not shown, not run, no button to click by mistake.

Checked on your PC

Even if my site were hacked

Nothing harmful could be made to run. Whoever took over the site would still need my private key, which never leaves my machine. That is the whole reason the checking happens on your computer rather than mine.

By design

Nothing runs in the background

It listens only while you have it connected, and only on your own computer — nobody on your network or the internet can reach it. A web page on any other site cannot drive it either.

Local only

You can read every line of it

The source is public. Not open source — you cannot take it and republish it — but published so that anyone asked to install it can check what it does first. If you are the sort of person who checks, please do.

On GitHub

What a signature does not promise. It proves who published a tool and that nobody altered it. It does not promise the tool is right for your model. It is free software with no warranty, it changes live models, and you remain responsible for your own work — so keep backups and check the results before you rely on them. The full terms are shown to you during install, before anything is written to your computer.

Getting it running

Five steps, once. After that you never do this again.

1 · Download and extract

Take the zip from the button above and extract all of it to a folder. Not just part of it, and do not run it from inside the zip.

2 · Close Revit

If Revit is open while you install, the new tab will not appear until you restart it anyway. Easier to close it first.

3 · Double-click Install.cmd

It shows the terms, waits for you, then installs. If Windows warns about an unrecognised file, choose More info and then Run anyway — that warning appears for anything downloaded, not because something is wrong.

4 · Start Revit and open a model

There will be a new AJ Connect tab on the ribbon. Nothing else of mine is installed — no other tabs, no other add-ins.

5 · Press AJ Connect, then Connect

Your browser opens with the tools. Click one and it works on your model. That is it.

If something looks wrong

No tab? Revit was probably open during the install — close it fully and start again. Browser will not load the page? Press the ribbon button again and let it open the browser for you; the address needs its port number on the end.

Removing it is one line. Run install-connector.ps1 -Uninstall from the same folder. It removes only AJ Connect — every other add-in you have, including AJ‑Tools itself, is left alone.
