Heron AI

Say what you want. Get it done, in the model.

Heron AI lets a BIM modeller, BIM coordinator or engineer drive complex Revit automation by describing the work in ordinary language.

“What’s missing before I issue this?”
“Did anything come apart? Check the ductwork.”
“Which unit feeds this? Follow the pipe.”
“Work out the air for this room, then lay out the terminals.”

Behind that sentence, Heron performs the analysis, knowledge retrieval, code generation, validation, testing, execution, logging and learning. The user never has to know C#, the Revit API, MCP, RAG, vector databases, build systems or version compatibility.

It is not a chatbot, a coding assistant, or a plain MCP server. It is a platform that turns a sentence into a checked, reversible, logged change in a real model — or into a straight answer, and nothing else.

What is actually there 51 tools · 45 of them can only look

The Heron tab 5 buttons

Everything a person presses. Two of them answer a question a picture answers faster than a menu; three are the permissions, and each one is a padlock you can see across the room.

  • HeronConnect or disconnect this Revit session. Click again to reverse it. The picture is the state — lit when connected, dark when not — so “am I connected?” is answered without clicking anything.
  • Bridge StatusWhether the bridge is running, and on which pipe. The detail you only want when something looks wrong, so it sits under the arrow instead of taking a button of its own.
  • ChangesWhether Heron may change this model at all. Closed padlock: it can read and nothing else. Open padlock: it may change it. Turning it on asks first; turning it off never does.
  • AdminWhether Heron may change how the project itself is set up — project parameters, templates, the things a model is built on rather than the things in it. Off unless you say otherwise.
  • PublishWhether Heron may send anything out of the model — export, print, save, sync with central. Separate from Changes on purpose: reading and writing a model is not the same act as putting a copy of it somewhere.
Five buttons, and that is the whole ribbon

A Revit add-in usually puts a button on the ribbon for every job it does, because pressing the button is how you ask. Heron is the other way round — you say what you want, so there is nothing to press. The only things that earn a button are the two questions a picture answers faster than a menu, am I connected and can this thing change my model, and the two further padlocks under it.

Everything in the list below runs in the chat and on your own PC, on the far side of the bridge — not inside Revit.

How the pieces fit 3 words

A fragment is one reusable unit of implementation — C# plus the notes that describe it. It is the thing that actually does the work. A capability is what you want done, named without naming who does it. A skill is what you can ask for in your own words. Skills name capabilities, never fragments — so a fragment can be replaced, split or retired without a single skill changing.

What “proven” means here 534 of 605

A compile is not a proof. A passing test is not a proof. A proof is a recorded run against a named real model, with a negative case and a fingerprint that goes stale the moment the code changes underneath it. 329 fragments have one. 66 do not, and stay marked draft until they do — including every fragment re-authored from Ajmal’s earlier libraries, which arrives here unproven no matter how well it worked where it came from.

How it is built four parts, and only one of them is inside Revit

The four parts 4

  • Heron RevitThe add-in, the ribbon, and everything that touches the Revit API. C#, running inside Revit itself — and deliberately the smallest of the four parts, because it is the only one that can bring Revit down with it.
  • Heron MCPThe controlled bridge between the AI layer and Revit. A named pipe on the same machine — never a network socket.
  • Heron BrainSkills, fragments, retrieval, memory and knowledge. Python, on your own PC.
  • Heron PlatformInstall, update, packages, registry, security. The layer underneath the other three.

What happens when you ask for something 5 steps

  1. You type a sentence“Select all ducts” — in ordinary words, in the chat.
  2. The brain works out what you meantWhich capability this needs, and which fragment currently provides it.
  3. The request crosses the bridgeDown a named pipe that exists only on your machine. No socket, no port, no internet.
  4. Revit does the work on its own threadThrough a single scheduled event — the only safe way to reach the Revit API without bringing Revit down.
  5. You see it on screenThe selection changes, or the answer comes back, and one line goes into the audit log.

Three kinds of agent 250

  • Tier 1 — service · 167Plain deterministic code. Never calls a language model at all. Runs in about a millisecond.
  • Tier 2 — reasoner · 63One tightly scoped model call with a strict contract. Hundreds of milliseconds.
  • Tier 3 — worker · 20A full agentic loop with tools. Seconds to minutes, and used sparingly.

Roughly two thirds of the agents never call a model at all, and that is deliberate. Once “select all ducts” has a proven fragment, the whole run should be ordinary code — the model is asked what you meant, then gets out of the way. Faster, repeatable, and the same question twice gives the same answer twice.

Why it is safe to point at a real model the part that matters most

Before it can change anything 4

  • Writing is off until you turn it onThere is a padlock button on the Heron ribbon tab. Closed, Heron can read the model and nothing else. It ships closed and stays closed until you open it.
  • It tells you what it is about to do“This will move 247 ducts up by 200 mm. 12 of them are owned by another user and will be skipped. Proceed?” — before anything moves.
  • The preview is the real run, undoneNot a simulation that could be wrong. Heron does the work for real inside a transaction and rolls it back, so what you are shown is what would actually have happened.
  • Seven permission levelsRead, analyse, suggest, execute, modify, publish, admin. The boundary that matters is between the ones that only look and the ones that change something.

While it is working 3

  • Everything it changes is one Ctrl+ZEvery modifying operation runs inside exactly one transaction group, named after what you asked for. Your undo stack reads “Heron: Move ducts 200 mm up” — one entry, not two hundred.
  • Revit tells you it is still aliveRevit runs one thing at a time on the thread that also draws the screen, so while Heron works Revit is genuinely frozen — and a frozen application with no explanation reads as a crash. A banner counts the seconds and says whether Heron is reading or changing, which is the real question.
  • A runaway script cannot take Revit downA fragment that recursed forever used to end Revit and the unsaved model with it. Now it comes back as an ordinary error with Revit still running. Proved in front of Revit on 16 September 2026.

Where your data goes 3

  • The part inside Revit opens no connectionsNo sockets, listening on no ports. It speaks over a named pipe to another process on the same machine, and that is the only way out of it. An IT department can be told that accurately, and check it.
  • Models never leave your machineRevit and family files are refused by file extension before they are even opened. Documents can be indexed; models and families go nowhere.
  • It will not invent a standardAny claim about ISO 19650, QCS, Ashghal or a company standard has to cite an indexed source document. No source, no claim — an uncited standards answer is treated as a bug, not as a low-confidence answer.

What has been proved, and on which Revit honestly

Every row here is a thing somebody watched happen in front of a real Revit, on a named date. Nothing on this page is called proven because it compiled.

  • The bridgeProven in Revit 2020 and 2024. One button connects; the thread hop counted 5,844 elements in a live 2024 model and 3,167 in 2020; “select all ducts” found four and highlighted them in both. With two Revits open it refused to guess, and stopped when the chosen one closed.
  • The first writeMeasured on one release. On 17 October 2026, three ducts in a copy of Autodesk’s MEP sample moved +200.0000 mm each in Revit 2024, read back two ways; a pinned duct beside them did not move; and one Ctrl+Z put them back. A forced failure mid-move and the other rails are still owed.
  • Keep or Discard, and the safety railsRun in Revit 2024 and 2027 on 18 October 2026, on the owner’s laptop, with Keep, Discard, Esc and Undo pressed by hand. The faults it found were logged, not hidden.
  • A fragment cannot end RevitProved in front of Revit on 16 September 2026. A runaway recursion comes back as a finding, with Revit still running.
  • The fragment libraryMostly proven, the rest draft. A draft is not “unwritten” — it is not proven as it stands: either new, or sent back when its code changed. The split is counted straight from the repository at the top of this page.
  • The skillsAll 35 are still draft. A skill is what you can ask for in your own words; none of them has yet been proved end to end against a real model.

How it is put together the short answer

  • HostA Claude Code plugin — skills, subagents, the MCP server and the Revit add-in.
  • Revit support2020 to the latest release, and every future one. Declared support and tested releases are two different lists, and heron_compatibility tells them apart rather than blurring them.
  • LanguagesC# for everything that touches Revit. Python for the brain and the MCP side.
  • TransportNamed pipes. The add-in is the server, and it is local-only by construction — not by configuration.
  • Revit threadingOne ExternalEvent, one request queue, one handler. The rule the Revit API actually imposes, obeyed in one place.
  • Generated codeC# compiled at run time inside Revit’s process. No fragment is production-compiled yet, so today every one — proven included — is compiled when it runs.
  • Licence and distributionApache 2.0, free and open source on public GitHub.
