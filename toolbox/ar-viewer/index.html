AR Viewer

See a Revit model in AR on your phone, at real size or as a table model. Choose an FBX export, check the size, then view it in AR: straight from Chrome on Android, or through a USDZ file on iPhone and iPad.

  • Free tool
  • Runs in your browser
  • No analytics

Your file stays on your device. It is opened and converted inside this browser tab. Nothing is uploaded, stored or shared, and this page runs no analytics.

The AR Viewer needs JavaScript and a recent browser: Safari on iOS 16.4 or newer, Chrome on Android, or a current Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari on a computer.

1. Choose your FBX

In Revit, open a 3D view and use File › Export › FBX. For a phone, export one level or zone at a time.

Starting AR…

What this does, and what it does not.

Scale. Revit writes FBX in feet, and the tool converts to metres, so real size in AR is real size. Check one known dimension before you rely on it. Table size and the scales shrink the model by that ratio.

Position. The model is centred and its lowest point sits on the floor. It is not placed at its site location or turned to face north.

Android. View in AR opens the camera in Chrome. Point the phone at the floor until the ring shows, then tap Place. The model locks where it stands; tap Move to slide or turn it. Chrome holds it with an AR anchor where the phone offers one. Phone AR is a visual check, not a survey.

iPhone and iPad. Create the USDZ, then tap View in AR. Quick Look places it on the floor; pinch there to resize it.

Colours. Only materials saved inside the FBX come across. An FBX with none shows plain grey, and textures stored on the computer that made the FBX are skipped.

Faces. AR draws each face from its front side only, the same as the preview. Mirrored families are turned the right way round first.

Speed. The model is joined into a few large meshes, one per material, so a phone draws it quickly. Thousands of Revit objects become a handful of meshes.

Size. In testing, a 27.6 MB Revit FBX made an 84 MB USDZ. If a phone struggles, create the USDZ on a computer, send it to the phone, and open it from the Files app.

Built with three.js 0.186.1 (FBXLoader, USDZExporter and WebXR), loaded from jsDelivr. The Android AR follows the approach of BIM2AR. AR opens in Safari on iPhone and iPad with iOS 16.4 or newer, and in Chrome on Android phones with Google Play Services for AR. Built by Ajmal P.S, MEP BIM Coordinator in Doha, Qatar.
